Questions? Answers.
Expand all 
Is the comparison with SONIC fair?
The two runs differ in data and hardware: SONIC trained on 128 NVIDIA L40 GPUs, SuperMimic on one NVIDIA H100. Neither difference explains the gap. SuperMimic trains on 264.56 hours of BONES-SEED, the public subset of SONIC’s 611 hours: 
2.3× less data
. An H100 delivers 0.74× the fp32 and 5.5× the tensor-core peak of an L40, and how much of that peak either run used is unknown.
2.3× in data and at most 5.5× in peak GPU throughput cannot account for a 1000× gap in GPU-hours.
Does SuperMimic need a differentiable simulator?
No. 
SuperMimic never differentiates the simulator.
 It fits a world model to logged transitions of a standard rigid-body simulator, MuJoCo Warp, and takes analytic policy gradients through that learned model. Simulators with non-smooth contacts work as they are.
Is the world model trained first, then frozen?
No. 
The world model updates every iteration, alongside the policy.
 It is held fixed only inside each policy update.
What do the 3 hours include?
The full training loop:
 collecting simulator transitions, fitting the world model and updating the policy. Checkpoint evaluations run after training and are not counted.
The 3 hours are stated conservatively. From then on, every checkpoint is ahead of SONIC by at least half of the gap between SONIC and SuperMimic’s best result, in both return and failed motions.
What counts as a failed motion?
Each of the 129,514 motions plays once from its first frame, with the same simulation for every policy. 
A motion fails if the rollout triggers SuperMimic’s training termination.
What does the critic do?
Imagined rollouts are 32 steps long. 
A critic bootstraps the return beyond that horizon.
 With the critic, a 16-step horizon matches a critic-free 32-step one; without it, training fails at 16 steps or fewer.
Does the policy run on a real robot?
Yes. 
One policy runs on a physical Unitree G1
 in every real-robot clip on this page, with no per-motion tuning.
Normalized return is the undiscounted episode reward sum divided by its maximum, from 0 to 1. SONIC’s released checkpoint scores 0.90.
Compute accounting compares SONIC’s reported 128 NVIDIA L40 GPUs for 7 days (about 21,000 GPU-hours) with 3 hours on one NVIDIA H100, within which SuperMimic surpasses the return of SONIC’s released checkpoint. The ratios are about 7200× in GPU-hours and about 56× in wall-clock time, stated as more than 1000× and more than 50×. The 3 hours cover the full training loop; checkpoint evaluations are not counted.
Benchmark: 129,514 motions of the filtered BONES-SEED training set (264 hours). Each motion plays once from its first frame with foot friction 1.0, no domain randomization or observation noise, and deterministic physics. A motion fails if the rollout triggers SuperMimic’s training termination. SONIC: released checkpoint.
Measured on an H100. One step is one 50 Hz control step, which MuJoCo Warp computes as four 5 ms substeps; the world model runs at the TF32 precision used in SuperMimic’s training.
